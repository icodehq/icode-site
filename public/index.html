Meet Mira

A teammate who watches all your projects.

Mira is a person in the sidebar, not another project tab. One conversation spans every repository: she reads the real queue, answers “what needs me?”, and can create, cancel, retry, or archive work through validated actions.

She has no shell and no project working directory. She cannot approve a proposal, accept a review, merge, or push. Those stay human decisions.

  • She comes to you — approvals, failures, waiting tasks, and review-ready work arrive with a sidebar badge and an OS notification when no window is focused.
  • She acts with limits — low-risk tasks can auto-dispatch under a daily cap; protected paths and keywords wait for a person.
  • She remembers — durable preferences live in Settings, visible and removable.
How Mira works

Principles

The work stays yours.

Isolation

Agents do not share a checkout.

Every new session gets its own Git worktree and branch at the current HEAD. Two agents in the same repository never edit the same files by accident. Its sidebar row carries details, sleep or wake, restart, pin, and remove; removal names the work at risk and keeps the branch.

Judgment

Review is a gate, not a formality.

Task work shows its changes against a baseline, and a review decision is separate from process exit. Accepting records the judgment; it does not merge, push, or delete anything.

Local-first

Your machine holds the state.

Projects, sessions, worktrees, tasks, and schedules live in local SQLite. There is no telemetry and no hosted service; model traffic belongs to the harness you chose.

Start with the surface that fits.

Use Workbench when you want a fleet and a control room. Use Agent when you want one focused session in the terminal. Neither takes the judgment away from you.
